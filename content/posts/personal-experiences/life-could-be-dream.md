# Premise

I'll keep this brief: I really can't pick a hobby. I'd like to do everything.

I really like stories, games and music. I also really like cooking and computer hardware. And I can go on about the many things I really like. As children, we've always wanted to build things. We enjoyed the time we built lego sets. Or wrote our own lore for why Naruto is planet-level and Optimus Prime (*RIP Peter Cullen*) is higher than planet-level and therefore one shots Naruto in big-fox mode with purple emo armour, prep time and pervert powers.


I think that inner child is still in us, even if COVID or the job market has left us feeling dead on the inside. For me though, I still think I have it in me to make something special. But why stop at one? I'll be addressing this and other questions I've been asked by family and friends.


## What even is the point of it???

Why would I possibly want to make so many things? Because I'd like to. I understand that life is busy, skills take time to build, friends and family exist and we need time for ourselves. And each new skill/hobby would just pile up and leave no time for anything else or I'd probably just suck at it. It all boils down to:
1. I like spending time on them
2. Sharing these interests with people is nice
3. I will remember that I tried and got better at something


None of this matters if I can't keep up with the hobbies. I'm in the midst of figuring that out for myself, and fortunately, my line of work is naturally teaching me skills that would help me with some of my interests. More importantly, my lifestyle is changing. I've been trying to read, make improvements to my health, I'm talking to more people, practicing music more and I'm hoping to do more in the coming months. Maybe I can't do everything just yet, but I can do a lot and I'd regret not having at least tried. Plus, the things I'm doing are helping me out.


I also would like to collaborate with people to ease out the burden of having to do absolutely everything. I'd like to show commitment myself before I get to that and then team up with people who share my interests. This is the real answer on how I'll do everything. There's still a lot I can do myself. Look at Maynard Keenan, frontman of Tool. He sings for 3 bands, makes wine, does brazillian jujitsu, is a farmer, owns some restaurants, served in the military and does some movies/documentaries every now and then. And he's not the only one. There are several Renaissance Men in this world, proving that it really is possible to pull it off. 


*Note*: I think we are in the prelude of a new Renaissance era, and that there will be a new wave of talented people who can do many things really well.


## What about professionals or AI? Either one can certainly do a better job.
Every once in a while, I make food for the family. I still think the food sucks and isn't great, but they liked it. I brought some pickled onions and carrots I made to college and we devoured that in 5 minutes raw ... I'm still proud of it even if it wasn't much.


And I think that holds true for everything I do and I believe its true of everyone too. This is the one reason I believe we have to build things even if someone or something can do it better than we ever could. Its personal and it meant something. I'm sure one day, AI could write a version of Lord of the Rings that would be good. But it won't have Tolkiens love for Life and languages or his pain for the battles he fought. I doubt that the person writing it with AI actually cares about what they do, why else would they not do it themselves? There will be reasons I'm sure, maybe even valid ones. **However, it takes away from us the pleasure of trying and the joy of giving. That is why we must make things**.


Sometimes, the thing that is made doesn't last, but where possible, I should like to see that it does. I still have some gifts from my time abroad and away from home and look at them for nostalgia. *There's value in the making of a thing, the thing itself and the memory of it too.* Thats why it matters that I do it.
