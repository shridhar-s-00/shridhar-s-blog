# Where it all began

This goes in so many different directions so I'll break it down to 3 major categories: nostalgia, trying new things for myself and survival.

## Nostalgia
So, back in 2010-2012, there was this channel on television called Fox Life. It used to have a different name (I think it was fox traveller or something), but thats not important. It was one of a few channels I really enjoyed watching as a kid. The best parts were the cooking shows. Not that I actually ended up cooking, but there were a great many shows that actually showed me so many different cuisines from around the world. You'd see unconventional drinks, crazy food ideas, American food trucks, italian food from its many provinces, Sri Lankan food, snacks, desserts, cooking competitions (masterchef australia is still the best cooking show by a mile) and so much more.

For me, watching these shows kind of enforced only 1 thing: Food was the best source comfort in life. Its the solution to depression, anxiety, stress and so many other things. I no longer treat it as such because its extremely unhealthy, but theres still some truth to that I think. In my 15 years of learning to cook (starting at the age of 9-10), I've goofed up, quit, started again. Apart from wishing I'd been more disciplined at times, I do not regret learning to cook what I'd like to eat at all. In fact, 

I lived in Croydon when I was five and I'd get a lot of really good quality fruits from the market there. Truly, nothing like the many different varieties of apples, the kiwis, strawberries, oranges and so much more. Those times are gone, but its a somewhat well documented thing that memories can sometimes be very tightly linked to smell and taste. Every once in a while, I get the somewhat familiar smell of a specific flower. For a moment, I'm taken back to my time there and its sort of blissful.

## Survival

On 3 September 2023, I was admitted to the ICU after having extreme difficulty breathing, eating and sleeping for a few weeks. It was first diagnosed as some gastric issues, then a throat disease and something else. The medicines I got and the food I had to eat, I couldn't swallow at all. Not even water. For those few weeks before going to the ICUE, I craved to eat, to breathe, to drink, to sleep. Then, after being admitted, I managed to sleep somewhat peacefully, but not without first experiencing some of the worst pain I had experienced in my life. A week later, I was moved to the ward and was told that I had cancer. It was very curable and I had the strength to endure treatment, but I had food restrictions. Low salt, low spice, no raw food, limited pottasium intake and the list went on. I wasn't going to just let them stick needles and tubes into me without getting something in return for all that pain. No, I was going to have the food I liked no matter what and if my doctor gave me restrictions on what I can eat, then I can damn well fight my around those restrictions myself.

It started with ketchup. I couldn't buy the store bought stuff so I started making it myself. Then I made my own fried rice because mom didn't know how to make it. Then I made my own soup. And my own paneer butter masala/ channa masala. And I kept going. If nobody could help me get the food I wanted, I was going to make it when I had the time. And I had nothing but time for a year and so I got to work. Relaxations were present at times, so I could eat a little ice cream and little cheese once in a while and I made the most of it. Now that I've started working after completing treatment, I'm going to completely cut out the crappy junk food out of my life, and make it the way I like. 

I decided to take my lunch into my own hands. I leave home at around 6:30 in the morning and my mom has to juggle a few things. Once again, I had to take care of myself and now I've started cooking out of necessity.
